# Role sets the animal; the project sets the colour; you and the Queen are people

In the town, a character's animal comes from its role: a lead is a cat, an implementer a beaver,
an explorer a hare, a tester a hedgehog and a reviewer an owl. A subagent type that isn't one of
these is the nearest of the five, wearing its own type's hat. The project shows only in colour:
the lead's scarf and apron, a house's door and pennant, a slip's seal, a parcel's string. You and
the Queen are clay people, not animals, drawn a little taller than the folk.

Context: the town must let you tell the job from across the island ("Houses and characters in the
new look", the user's pick H3 Animal folk, "Yeah role set animal"). The user also asked that "I
should be a person, as should the queen".

We weighed two other ways to cast the folk:

- **Each project its own animal.** A project would be easy to spot, but every animal would be a
  lead in one place and a helper in another, so a role could only be read from its hat.
- **Random helper animals.** It is varied, but it says nothing.

Both lose "know the job from across the island", which is what the town is for.

## Consequences

- A new subagent type needs no new animal; it maps to the nearest of the five and keeps its hat.
  A sixth animal would be a look for the user to pick.
- Two projects that share a colour can only be told apart by place and name plate, as today.
- The avatar presets keep dressing you as a person ("the king in a crown, the rest hatless").
- Once the folk are built, changing the cast is a redraw of the kit, so this is hard to reverse.
