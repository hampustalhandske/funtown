# Funtown's goal

## Why

Running many Claude Code agents across many projects should feel like tending a town you
love: calm when things are fine, clear when they're not, and growing your judgement instead
of wearing it down.

Today that work means a pile of terminals. You read each one to find out what it's doing,
and you lose track of who needs you and where each project is heading.

Funtown plays like two games in one. Like a manager game, you pick the team, delegate and
choose how much of each match to watch. Like a cozy village game, you tend a place that
grows with what gets done.

## Who it's for

For the person who builds it first: one developer running many agents on a Mac. It's built
clean enough that any engineer who runs Claude Code could install it and feel at home.

## What success looks like

One glance tells you what needs you, what's burning and where each project is heading, and
every decision comes with what it means for your goals.

- **The three-second glance:** open the app on a real day, and within three seconds you can
  name what needs you and what's burning.
- **Answers go through Funtown:** over five working days, at least 90% of answers to waiting
  sessions are given in Funtown.
- **You want to open it:** at least once a day you open it without needing to, because it's
  a pleasant place to be.

## What it is not

- **Not a dashboard.** No board of numbers. The state lives in the world, and the detail is
  one key away.
- **Never hides the tech.** Real commands, real states and a plain list are always one key
  away. Engineers stay in control. The game dresses the facts and never hides them.
- **Not a cloud service.** It runs on your Mac. Nothing leaves it except what Claude Code and
  GitHub's tools already send, plus features you switch on.
- **Not required by any workflow.** Every project, spec and PR works the same with Funtown
  switched off, and it shows any Claude Code session, whether or not the session follows a
  workflow.

## Big parts

- **Glance:** the world tells the truth at a glance. A red PR is a burning house, a long
  wait shows in how a character stands, neglect grows weeds, and all green is calm. Only the
  most urgent thing is loud, and the town never looks calm when it doesn't know. A small
  panel in the corner, like a game's resource bar, shows the numbers worth knowing: usage
  left this week and in the current five-hour window, tokens spent, PRs merged today and in
  total, and open issues.
- **Desk:** whatever needs you (a question, a permission, the morning report, the Queen's
  review, a project's lead asking for a decision) is one click or one key away. It comes
  with the context to answer it well: which goal and spec it serves, the options and what
  each leads to, what the agent proposes and what the Queen suggests, and how serious the
  decision is. A project's lead walks to you with its questions and its finished PR. Short
  keyboard commands do the rest.
- **Yard:** the work is visible as work. Inside each project, the lead stands at its desk and
  moves when its attention shifts to a new stage, and the agents it sends out move between
  the jobs they're doing, from goal to merged PR. The house grows with what was finished in
  it. Movement only shows what the agents already report, so it never costs a token.
  Before a run starts, you can see its lineup (which agents, which models, in what order),
  set to the workflow's standard. You can change it if you're there, for example running one
  agent on a cheaper model or adding another builder.
- **Look:** fun to look at. The characters have personality, every house has its own shape,
  the island has a real landscape, and day and night follow your clock. It's inspired by
  cozy farm and village games, never copied from one.
- **Play:** small games for the moments you wait, which stop the moment someone needs you.
  Play is a feature, never the point.
- **Residents:** townsfolk who belong to no single project and act only when nothing needs
  you. Some are agents, such as a teacher who quizzes you on a project or a topic, or a
  scout who brings news of tools, models and ways of working. Some need no AI at all, such
  as one who hands you today's practice problem.
- **Life:** projects that do things for you rather than build code, such as job
  applications or interview practice, live in the town too. Each gets a house shaped by its
  work, and its urgency comes from dates. A project can bring its own practice into the town,
  such as fast mental-maths drills, so you can play them in Funtown.

Every project tells Funtown what to show (its stage, its dates, its practice) in one small,
standard form that Funtown reads directly, so showing a project never needs AI or costs
tokens.
- **App:** a native Mac app with a terminal that feels like the real one, for the times you
  still want it.

The Queen, the steward who knows your goals and taste, reviews pull requests for you and
comes to the Desk with what she finds. She sits beside you, and she keeps the castle when
you're away. She is the town's face for your global /steward.

## Later

- **Kontor:** other AI tools and connectors (Notion, for example) each get a building in the
  town, while staying compatible with how others develop.
- **Visitors:** other people walk around your town, see the agents at work and leave notes,
  but never act in your sessions.
